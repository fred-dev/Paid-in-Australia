# Governance

Paid in Australia is a collaborative policy development project with a fixed core thesis and open implementation details.

## Fixed commitments

The following commitments are not open to reversal through pull request.

1. Public spending should produce durable Australian public benefit.
2. Government funded markets should prefer regulated public-benefit suppliers where this is lawful, practical, and compatible with quality service delivery.
3. Where for-profit suppliers receive public money, policy should seek to keep a greater share of resulting profit connected to Australians.
4. Workers in government funded supply chains should have secure employment and fair wage structures.
5. The project should remain evidence led, legally serious, institutionally credible, and non-party-political.

## What can be changed

Contributors may propose changes to:

- definitions
- terminology
- evidence
- citations
- modelling assumptions
- risk analysis
- legal pathways
- implementation stages
- sector priorities
- procurement mechanisms
- enforcement mechanisms
- threshold values
- wording and public explanation

## What cannot be changed

Pull requests should not:

- convert the project into a general argument for privatisation
- convert the project into a general argument for nationalisation
- remove the public-benefit supplier concept
- remove the circular Australian-benefit ownership concept
- make the project party-political
- replace evidence with slogans
- present untested claims as settled fact
- remove the need for legal, economic, and sector expert review

## Decision model

During the early MVP stage, maintainers may merge changes that improve clarity, evidence, structure, or technical quality.

At later stages, pull requests should be assessed through three streams:

1. Community review, including transparent discussion and voting.
2. LLM assisted review, used to identify contradictions, missing implications, and consistency with project commitments.
3. Expert review, including legal, procurement, economic, labour, superannuation, and sector specific assessment.

LLM review must not replace expert judgement. It should be treated as an analytical aid.

## Treatment of contested questions

Where evidence is mixed or uncertain, documents should state that directly. The repository should keep separate sections for:

- evidence
- assumptions
- claims
- modelling inputs
- unresolved legal questions
- political or implementation risks

## Enforcement language

The project does not propose imprisonment as a default response to regulatory breach.

The preferred enforcement model is graduated and legally conventional. It may include warning notices, enforceable undertakings, repayment orders, civil penalties, director disqualification, procurement exclusion, loss of Public-Benefit Supplier status, and referral to existing criminal law processes only where conduct may already constitute an offence, such as fraud, false accounting, wage theft, bribery, corruption, or deliberate evasion.
