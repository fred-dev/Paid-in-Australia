# Paid in Australia

Paid in Australia is a collaborative policy development repository for proposals that seek to keep the benefits of Australian public spending circulating within Australia.

The project begins from a simple principle: when public money is spent, the public should receive more than the immediate contracted service. Public spending should strengthen Australian workers, Australian communities, Australian productive capacity, Australian superannuation, and long term national resilience.

Paid in Australia does not propose that government should own or operate every service. It does not reject competition, enterprise, innovation, specialist expertise, or private initiative. It asks whether government funded markets should be designed so that public money produces durable public benefit, rather than being extracted through excessive profit taking, offshore ownership, insecure labour, inflated corporate overheads, or opaque subcontracting chains.

## Core proposal

Paid in Australia proposes two linked policy streams.

First, create a new regulated status called a Public-Benefit Supplier. This is not charity status. Many useful government suppliers will not have a charitable purpose and should not need to register with the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission. A Public-Benefit Supplier would instead be a purpose built statutory supplier status for organisations that receive public money and accept stronger obligations in return.

A Public-Benefit Supplier would be required to reinvest surpluses into approved public-benefit purposes, such as service quality, workforce development, lower prices, regional access, equipment, training, local capacity, or future service resilience. It would be subject to restrictions on profit distribution, executive remuneration, subcontracting leakage, employment insecurity, related party transactions, and opaque accounting.

Second, create an Australian-benefit ownership pathway for for-profit suppliers. Where government contracts are awarded to for-profit companies, policy should encourage or require structures that keep a greater share of resulting profits connected to Australians. This may include Australian superannuation ownership, worker ownership, mutual ownership, sovereign backed co-investment, or other mechanisms that keep public spending circulating through the Australian economy.

## What this repository is for

This repository is an MVP for a long term policy project. It is intended to gather evidence, refine definitions, test objections, develop modelling, and eventually produce clear policy and legislative drafting for local, state, territory, and federal adoption.

The first version focuses on structure rather than final law. Later versions should include detailed legal drafting, modelling, sector specific implementation plans, and clause by clause amendments.

## Project status

This is an early policy development repository. It is not legal advice, financial advice, procurement advice, or investment advice. It is a working policy proposal that requires expert review.

## Repository structure

- `docs/` contains the core policy documents.
- `proposals/` contains early-adoption and later-stage proposal notes. Early sectors are grouped in a single proposal so the project does not imply that childcare, NDIS, education, or waste removal are the only target areas.
- `modelling/` contains the modelling plan, assumptions, scenarios, and future data work.
- `references/` contains source notes and a bibliography.
- `templates/` contains contribution templates.


## Current proposal files

The MVP proposal section currently contains:

- `early-adoption-sectors.md`, covering childcare, education-related services, NDIS and disability services, and waste removal as candidate early pilots, while inviting submissions for additional sectors.
- `consulting-and-professional-services.md`, treating consultancy as a major later-stage reform area.
- `superannuation-backed-supplier-ownership.md`, covering the Australian-benefit ownership pathway for for-profit suppliers.

## Core commitments

The project has five fixed commitments.

1. Public spending should produce durable Australian public benefit.
2. Government funded markets should prefer suppliers that reinvest surpluses into public purposes where this is lawful, practical, and consistent with quality service delivery.
3. Where for-profit suppliers receive public money, policy should seek to keep a greater share of resulting profit within Australia.
4. Workers in government funded supply chains should have secure employment and fair wage structures.
5. The project should remain legally serious, evidence led, non-party-political, and open to scrutiny.

## Contribution model

Pull requests are welcome. Contributions should improve the evidence, definitions, drafting, implementation pathways, modelling, legal analysis, or public explanation of the proposal.

Pull requests should not change the foundational purpose of the project into general privatisation, nationalisation, deregulation, austerity, charity reform, or party-political campaigning.
