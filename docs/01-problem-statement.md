# Problem Statement

Australian governments spend large sums through markets that are created, sustained, or heavily shaped by public funding.

In many of these markets, the government does not directly provide the service. It purchases the service, subsidises the user, or funds a provider market. This model can produce competition and innovation, but it can also create leakage of public value.

Commonwealth AusTender reported $104.9 billion in contract value for 2024-25. Of that, 60.5% of new contract value was in contracts over $20 million. Services represented 74.3% of total value. 88.3% went to suppliers with an Australian address — but Australian address is not the same as Australian beneficial ownership, Australian tax residency, or Australian profit circulation.

## Forms of leakage

Public value can be lost through:

- excessive profit extraction
- offshore ownership or dividend flows
- high executive remuneration
- layered management overheads
- opaque subcontracting chains
- related party transactions
- insecure employment
- aggressive cost cutting that damages service quality
- underinvestment in workforce capability
- thin accountability once public money enters private structures

## The policy question

The central question is not whether government should do everything itself. The central question is whether public money should be allowed to flow through supplier structures that extract value without strong obligations to reinvest in Australian public benefit.

Paid in Australia proposes that government funded markets should be designed around public-benefit obligations.

## Why this matters

If public money is used to create a market, the design of that market is a public policy choice. Governments can set supplier eligibility, reporting obligations, procurement scoring, workforce standards, ownership transparency, and reinvestment requirements.

The proposal is to use these powers more deliberately so that public spending circulates through Australian workers, communities, public-benefit institutions, and long term national savings.
