# Staged Implementation

The policy should be implemented gradually. The aim is to build capability, test legal design, and avoid service disruption.

## Stage 0: Research and consultation

- Map existing government funded markets.
- Identify sectors with high leakage risk.
- Review procurement constraints.
- Review superannuation law constraints.
- Review existing not-for-profit and mutual structures.
- Consult workers, service users, unions, providers, superannuation funds, procurement officials, legal experts, and regulators.

## Stage 1: Transparency

Introduce reporting requirements for large government suppliers, including ownership, subcontracting, related party payments, executive remuneration, labour standards, offshore payments, and reinvestment.

## Stage 2: Voluntary registration

Create a voluntary Public-Benefit Supplier register.

Early registrants should receive technical support, model documents, procurement onboarding, and access to pilot opportunities.

## Stage 3: Procurement scoring pilots

Apply procurement scoring preferences in selected sectors. The first pilots should use clear evaluation criteria and public reporting.

Candidate early-adoption sectors:

- early childhood education and care
- education-related services
- NDIS and disability services
- waste removal and local government services

These are starting examples only. The early-adoption proposal invites submissions for other sectors that may be suitable for pilots. Consulting and professional services should be treated as a later-stage reform because the spending is significant but the procurement, panel, confidentiality, capability-transfer, and legal issues are more complex.

## Stage 4: Formation support

Create subsidised pathways for skilled workers and local operators to form Public-Benefit Suppliers.

Support should include legal templates, startup loans, compliance support, insurance assistance, payroll support, procurement readiness, and shared back office services.

## Stage 5: Sector mandates

After pilots and review, specific sectors may move from preference to mandate. This should occur only where service continuity, competition, and legal compliance can be maintained.

## Stage 6: Federal legislative package

Develop federal legislation to create the Public-Benefit Supplier status, establish the regulator or registration function, define compliance obligations, and amend procurement settings.

## Stage 7: Modelling and evaluation

Publish ongoing modelling on:

- public value retained
- wage effects
- service quality
- administrative costs
- supplier entry and exit
- regional access
- superannuation impacts
- transition costs
- fiscal effects
