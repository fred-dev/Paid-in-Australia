# Risks and Objections

This document records serious objections and design risks. A credible policy must face these directly.

## Objection: This is protectionism

Response: The policy should be framed as public-benefit procurement, domestic value retention, transparency, workforce security, and long term public value. However, hard domestic ownership rules may raise procurement and trade law issues. This is why the MVP begins with transparency, procurement scoring, and incentives before mandates.

## Objection: This is socialism

Response: The proposal does not require government to own or operate the suppliers. It preserves competition between independent suppliers. The difference is that suppliers receiving public money would accept public-benefit obligations.

## Objection: This will reduce competition

Response: This is a real risk. Implementation should be staged, sector specific, and monitored. The policy should include formation support so skilled workers and local operators can create new suppliers.

## Objection: Not-for-profit suppliers can also be inefficient

Response: Correct. The proposal should not assume that every not-for-distribution structure is efficient or high quality. Public-Benefit Suppliers should be assessed on service quality, cost, transparency, workforce standards, and public-benefit outcomes.

## Objection: Public-Benefit Suppliers may use subcontracting to avoid the rules

Response: The policy should include subcontracting caps, related party transaction reporting, audit powers, and loss of status for evasion.

## Objection: Salary caps may deter skilled leaders

Response: The proposed cap is high enough to attract senior expertise while preventing extreme executive extraction from public funds. The exact threshold should be modelled and indexed.

## Objection: A ban on casual employment may reduce flexibility

Response: The default should be secure employment for ordinary ongoing work. Narrow exceptions may be needed for genuinely irregular, emergency, seasonal, student, trial, or short term work. These exceptions should be auditable.

## Objection: Superannuation funds cannot be forced to invest for political reasons

Response: This is a central legal and fiduciary constraint. Any superannuation pathway must serve member interests, meet risk and return standards, maintain diversification, and comply with trustee duties. The MVP therefore begins with incentives and investment vehicles rather than forced uneconomic investment.

## Objection: Ownership requirements may breach procurement commitments

Response: This requires legal review. The MVP separates lower risk transparency and scoring mechanisms from higher risk hard mandates.

## Objection: The policy may create compliance burden

Response: Compliance burden should be proportionate to contract size and risk. Small suppliers should receive model documents, shared services, and simplified reporting.

## Objection: The policy could create sham public-benefit entities

Response: This is a known risk. The status must include audit powers, related party rules, beneficial ownership transparency, subcontracting limits, and meaningful penalties.
