# Draft Legislative Pathways

This document is a placeholder for later legal drafting. It is not legal advice.

## Possible federal pathways

1. Amend Commonwealth procurement policy to recognise Public-Benefit Supplier status.
2. Create a Public-Benefit Supplier Act.
3. Establish a Public-Benefit Supplier register.
4. Define public-benefit obligations for registered suppliers.
5. Create reporting, audit, and enforcement powers.
6. Enable sector specific schemes to require or preference Public-Benefit Suppliers.
7. Create formation support and transition funding.
8. Introduce transparency requirements for large government suppliers.
9. Create Australian-benefit ownership disclosure rules.
10. Establish lawful incentive pathways for Australian-benefit ownership.

## Possible state and territory pathways

1. Amend procurement policies and guidelines.
2. Create pilot procurement categories.
3. Apply Public-Benefit Supplier preference to state funded services.
4. Support local formation of suppliers.
5. Align public-benefit status with state grants and service contracts.

## Possible local government pathways

1. Pilot public-benefit preference in maintenance and community services.
2. Require ownership and subcontracting transparency.
3. Support local worker led supplier formation.
4. Use local procurement policies to test public-benefit scoring.

## Drafting principles

Legislative drafting should:

- avoid charity law dependence
- define the supplier status clearly
- include proportionate obligations
- avoid unintended exclusion of small suppliers
- include transition periods
- provide appeal and review rights
- align with procurement commitments
- protect service continuity
- avoid forced uneconomic superannuation investment
