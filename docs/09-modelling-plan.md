# Modelling Plan

The project should eventually include economic modelling. The MVP should define the questions and required data before attempting results.

## Main modelling questions

1. How much public value leaks from government funded markets through profit extraction, offshore flows, high overheads, and subcontracting chains?
2. What proportion of this value could be retained through Public-Benefit Supplier structures?
3. What are the likely effects on wages, job security, prices, service quality, and provider entry?
4. What transition costs would arise if skilled workers form new Public-Benefit Suppliers?
5. What are the effects of Australian-benefit ownership on superannuation members, public value retention, and supplier financing?
6. What sectors are most suitable for early implementation?

## Core variables

- annual government expenditure by sector
- provider type
- provider ownership
- profit margin
- executive remuneration
- subcontracting spend
- labour share of revenue
- casual employment share
- offshore payments
- dividend distributions
- tax paid in Australia
- administrative overhead
- service quality indicators
- user outcomes
- worker turnover
- startup costs for new suppliers

## Scenario types

### Baseline

Current provider mix and expenditure.

### Transparency only

No supplier preference, but ownership, subcontracting, remuneration, and profit leakage reporting is required.

### Procurement preference

Public-Benefit Suppliers receive additional scoring weight in selected procurements.

### Sector pilot

A selected sector applies Public-Benefit Supplier preference to a defined percentage of contracts.

### Mandated public-benefit share

A sector requires a minimum share of government funded supply to be delivered by Public-Benefit Suppliers.

### Australian-benefit ownership pathway

For-profit suppliers receive scoring or incentive benefits for qualifying Australian-benefit ownership structures.

## Outputs

The model should estimate:

- value retained in Australia
- value reinvested into services
- wage effects
- employment security effects
- administrative cost changes
- provider entry and exit
- service disruption risk
- fiscal cost to government
- effect on superannuation members
- regional effects
