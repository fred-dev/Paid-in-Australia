# Definitions

This document defines working terms for the MVP. These definitions should be refined through legal and policy review.

## Public-Benefit Supplier

A Public-Benefit Supplier is a proposed new regulated status for organisations that supply government funded goods, services, works, care, professional services, or infrastructure support while accepting statutory public-benefit obligations.

A Public-Benefit Supplier is not automatically a charity and should not be required to have a charitable purpose.

## Eligible Public-Benefit Supplier

An Eligible Public-Benefit Supplier is a Public-Benefit Supplier that meets all registration, reporting, governance, employment, reinvestment, remuneration, and subcontracting requirements for a specific procurement or funding stream.

## Public-benefit purpose

A public-benefit purpose is an approved purpose connected to the public value of the funded activity. It may include:

- improved service quality
- workforce development
- training
- equipment
- regional access
- lower user costs
- improved continuity of care or service
- local productive capacity
- reserves for service resilience
- research, evaluation, and quality improvement

Public-benefit purpose is not the same as charitable purpose.

## Surplus

Surplus means revenue remaining after reasonable operating costs, wages, reserves, tax obligations where applicable, and approved expenditure.

For a Public-Benefit Supplier, surplus must be reinvested into approved public-benefit purposes and must not be distributed to members, directors, founders, related parties, or private investors.

## Profit leakage

Profit leakage means public money leaving the funded activity through private profit distribution, offshore dividend flows, excessive executive remuneration, related party transactions, inflated management fees, or other extraction mechanisms that do not materially improve the public service or public-benefit purpose.

## Subcontracting leakage

Subcontracting leakage means the use of subcontracting to shift public money into structures that avoid Public-Benefit Supplier obligations.

## Subcontracting cap

A subcontracting cap is a maximum percentage of total outgoing expenditure that may be spent on subcontractors, subject to sector specific exceptions for specialist work, emergency work, regional constraints, or approved transitional arrangements.

## Remuneration cap

A remuneration cap is the maximum total annual remuneration that may be paid to any worker, executive, contractor, or responsible person within a Public-Benefit Supplier. The working MVP figure is AUD 900,000 per year, subject to later evidence, indexing design, and sector review.

## Pay ratio

A pay ratio is the maximum permitted ratio between the highest and lowest paid workers in the organisation, calculated on a full time equivalent basis.

## Secure employment

Secure employment means that ordinary ongoing work should be performed through permanent full time or permanent part time employment. Casual employment should not be used for ordinary ongoing work by a Public-Benefit Supplier.

Limited exceptions may be required for genuinely irregular, emergency, seasonal, student, trial, or short term work. These exceptions should be narrow and auditable.

## Australian-benefit ownership

Australian-benefit ownership means ownership structures that keep a significant share of profits, dividends, capital growth, or long term value connected to Australians. This may include Australian superannuation funds, worker ownership, mutual ownership, community ownership, sovereign backed vehicles, or other regulated structures.

## Government funded market

A government funded market is a market created, sustained, guaranteed, subsidised, or materially shaped by public expenditure, procurement, voucher funding, grants, rebates, statutory schemes, or regulated user payments.
