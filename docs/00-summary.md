# Summary

Paid in Australia is a policy proposal for redesigning government funded markets so that public money produces durable Australian public benefit.

The proposal has two main streams.

## Stream A: Public-Benefit Suppliers

Create a new regulated supplier status called a Public-Benefit Supplier.

This is not charity status. A Public-Benefit Supplier would not need to have a charitable purpose. It would be a new statutory status for organisations that supply government funded services and agree to stronger public-benefit obligations.

These obligations may include:

- no private distribution of profits or surpluses
- reinvestment of surpluses into approved public-benefit purposes
- executive remuneration limits
- a maximum ratio between highest and lowest paid workers
- limits on subcontracting leakage
- secure employment requirements
- transparent reporting
- restrictions on related party transactions
- loss of status for repeated or serious non-compliance

The goal is to encourage skilled workers, professionals, and local operators to form leaner public-benefit organisations that can compete for government funded work without the overhead and extraction structures of large corporations.

## Stream B: Australian-benefit ownership

Where government contracts are awarded to for-profit companies, policy should seek to keep a greater share of resulting profit connected to Australians.

This may include Australian superannuation ownership, worker ownership, mutual ownership, sovereign backed co-investment, or other lawful mechanisms.

The immediate MVP does not assume that a hard ownership mandate is legally simple. It treats the ownership pathway as a staged proposal requiring detailed review under procurement law, superannuation law, competition law, corporations law, constitutional law, and trade commitments.

## Staged implementation

The policy should begin with low risk steps:

1. transparency requirements
2. voluntary Public-Benefit Supplier registration
3. procurement scoring pilots
4. sector specific pilots
5. subsidised formation pathways
6. model rules and constitutions
7. transition support for skilled workers
8. later legislative amendments where evidence supports them

Initial candidate sectors include early childhood education and care, education-related services, NDIS and disability services, and waste removal or other local government services. These are starting points for consultation, not a closed list. Consulting and professional services remain a major longer-term target because of the scale of government spending and the complexity of procurement panels, conflicts, capability transfer, and disclosure.
